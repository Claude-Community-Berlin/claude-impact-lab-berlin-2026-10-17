## README one-liners (the rules require these)

- **Who it's for:** immigrant parents in Germany whose child attends a Kita and who struggle with German and with how Kitas work.
- **Their problem today:** a Kita's rules, letters and events reach parents in German and assume local habits (Eingewöhnung, Laternenfest, the sick-child rules). Parents miss things, feel judged and are afraid to ask. General AI chatbots guess, because they know nothing about *this* Kita.
- **What the demo lets them do:** see their Kita's week in their own language, ask any question and get an answer based on the Kita's own documents, and send the Kita a polite German message drafted by Claude.

## Problem Statement

Amira moved from Syria to Berlin. Her son Yusuf (3) just started at Kita Sonnenblume. She gets a 6-page German handbook, paper letters and WhatsApp-style notes. She doesn't know:
- that a child with a fever must stay home for 24 hours *after* it goes,
- what "Eingewöhnung" means, or why she has to stay at the Kita for two weeks,
- that "Laternenfest" means making a lantern at home,
- how to tell the teacher, politely and in German, that Yusuf will be late.

Today she asks other parents, guesses, or stays silent. The cost: missed events, a child sent home, and feeling like an outsider.

## Impact (40 of 100 points: keep this front and centre)

- Real, frequent, emotional pain: Germany has hundreds of thousands of children from immigrant families in Kitas.
- It changes behaviour: the parent *acts* (brings the right thing, keeps a sick child home correctly, writes to the teacher) instead of just understanding.
- Trust: answers come only from the Kita's own documents, cite the source and say "ask the Kita" when the answer isn't in them. No invented rules.
- Inclusion: the parent's first message to the teacher in correct German is a moment of dignity. Make it the emotional peak of the demo.

## Premises (agreed)

1. One parent and one **fake** Kita ("Kita Sonnenblume"). Its document pack is the "centralized data", and it's sample data, as the rules require.
2. Answers come only from that Kita's documents, in the parent's language, with a short cultural note and the source.
3. "Talking to the Kita" means Claude drafts a German message the parent copies and sends. There's no app for Kita staff and no real messaging.
4. Out of scope: Kita staff features, several Kitas, accounts, real personal data and push reminders.

### The three features (in order of importance)

1. **Ask anything** (the core). The chat answers from the Kita documents in the chosen language, with: the answer, what to do, a 💡 cultural note when useful, and 📄 the source. If the answer isn't in the documents: "I don't know this from your Kita's documents. Ask the teacher; here's a message you can send."
2. **Write to the Kita.** The parent types what they want to say in their own language (e.g. "Yusuf will be late tomorrow, doctor appointment"). Claude returns a polite German message plus a back-translation, so the parent knows what they're sending. A copy button.
3. **This week board.** One Claude call reads the documents and the "today" date and returns JSON: events, deadlines, things to bring, cultural notes, in the chosen language. Cache it per language.

### Tech (keep it simple, per the rules)

- **Backend:** Python + FastAPI (or Flask), with 3 endpoints: `POST /ask`, `POST /draft-message`, `GET /week?lang=`.
- **Front end:** one `index.html` with plain JS and simple CSS. Two columns, readable on video.
- **AI:** Anthropic API, `claude-sonnet-5-5`. All Kita documents fit in the system prompt (they're small), so there's no RAG or vector database.
- **Repo layout:**
  ```
  data/kita-sonnenblume/   handbook.md, house-rules.md, letters/*.md, calendar.md   (all fake)
  prompts/                 ask.md, draft_message.md, week.md
  app.py                   FastAPI, reads ANTHROPIC_API_KEY from .env
  static/index.html
  .env.example  .gitignore (.env, node_modules)  README.md
  ```
- **Errors:** if the API call fails, show a friendly message ("Something went wrong, please try again in a moment"), as the rules require.

### Prompt sketch: `prompts/ask.md`

> You are a kind helper for an immigrant parent whose child attends Kita Sonnenblume in Berlin. Answer ONLY using the Kita documents below. Reply in {language}, in simple words (A2 level).
> Format: 1) a short answer, 2) "What to do" (if anything), 3) 💡 a cultural note if a German custom is involved, 4) 📄 the source document and section.
> If the documents don't contain the answer, say so clearly, suggest asking the teacher, and offer to draft the message. Never invent rules, dates or prices. Never give medical advice; for health questions, quote the Kita rule and suggest a doctor.
> <documents>{all docs}</documents>

## Approaches Considered

- **A: Web chat + Kita board** (chosen). The most visual for the 2-minute video, about 2.5h of build.
- **B: Telegram bot.** Closer to how parents live day to day, but harder to show on video, with more setup.
- **C: Chat only.** Fastest, but less to show and less of a "platform" feel.

## Two-minute video script

1. (15s) Amira's story: "Yusuf just started Kita. Everything is in German."
2. (25s) The week board: "Lantern walk Thursday, bring a lantern 💡 here's what that tradition is."
3. (30s) Ask: "My son had a fever last night, can he go tomorrow?" The answer quotes the 24h rule and its source.
4. (10s) Switch the language to Arabic; the same answer appears in Arabic.
5. (30s) "Write to the Kita": she types in her language, gets a polite German message plus a back-translation, and copies it.
6. (10s) Impact line: "Every parent belongs at their child's Kita, whatever language they speak."
